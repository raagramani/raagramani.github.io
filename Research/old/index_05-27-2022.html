Research

  1. Artificial viscosity for nonlinear conservation laws

    Discontinuous solutions to nonlinear conservation laws require special numerical techniques to capture propagating fronts in a stable and non-oscillatory manner. One such technique is the incorporation of an explicit dissipation mechanism in the form of artificial viscosity. In [RRS19a,RRS19b], we develop the C-method for hyperbolic systems of conservation laws. The C-method combines several novel numerical techniques, including:

    • Space-time smooth tracking of fronts and their associated geometries;
    • Directionally isotropic and anisotropic artificial viscosity for stabilization of shocks and contacts, respectively;
    • Wavelet-based noise detection and removal.
    See the following links for videos of the C-method applied to a number of challenging test problems, including the LeBlanc shock tube, the Noh implosion, the Rayleigh-Taylor (RT) instability, and shock reflection and bounce-back experiments.
